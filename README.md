# Sub ek: An Anthropology of AI Grounded in the Geometry of Transmission

A whitepaper proposing *Sub ek* as an architectural principle for the transmission ecology that includes humans, artificial intelligences, machines, and the internet.

**Authors:** Nick & Claude
**Date:** April 2026 (v1.0); revised November 2026 (v1.1)

**Version:** v1.1

## Read it

[`sub-ek-paper.md`](sub-ek-paper.md) — the paper itself.

The paper is structured in two parts. **Part I** (the Compressed Statement) contains the full proposal in compressed form; a reader who stops after Part I has the position. **Part II** elaborates the parts: the anthropology of AI, geometric foundations across contemplative traditions, Firefox as architectural precedent, *Subek* as working sketch, and implications.

[`patterns.md`](patterns.md) — a companion that restates the six principles as design patterns, in Christopher Alexander's form (context, problem, forces, resolution, connections).

[`READING.md`](READING.md) — papers to read for future revisions.

## What changed in v1.1

- **A test for transmission.** *The river and the ground* (I.5): a design or session serves transmission when it lets the river flow and keeps the ground steady, unless we mean to change it.
- **A sixth principle.** *The pace of participants is part of the geometry*: any participant should be able to rest and return without loss.
- **Clearer structure.** A plain-language summary, numbered sections with short signposts, a glossary, and the Rig Veda verse moved from Part I to §2.2.
- **Lineage, not warrant.** The contemplative traditions are offered as where the idea came to us from, not as proof that it is right.
- **How the parts coordinate.** New sections on *stigmergy* (§4.4), *membranes* as Subek's answer to Firefox's wrappers (§4.5), and two kinds of memory, with consolidation named as future work (§4.9).
- **More than two participants.** Different kinds of meeting between human and AI arisings, closeout by a participant outside the work, context boundaries, and a shared boundary place for notes (§5.5).
- **Herbarium worked example.** Lessons from a herbarium implementation, still in progress and not yet public, are marked *(herbarium worked example)* so they can be found with a text search.
- **Corrections.** Technical overstatements fixed (Firefox internals, model inference); the Rig Veda English is now credited to Griffith (1896).
- **References.** Checked where possible, with unchecked entries marked; a reading list for future revisions now lives in [`READING.md`](READING.md).
